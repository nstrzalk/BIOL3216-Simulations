Inside the model

V1 separates what the body requires from what the environment permits. The third quantity, actual evaporation, is an idealized assumption. The main screen contains exactly four model controls.

Fixed assumptions

A reference 70 kg adult with 1.8 m² body surface area, skin at 33°C, 0.5 clo light clothing, adequate hydration, standing in air at sea-level pressure. Radiant surroundings equal air temperature; no sun. Body mass is descriptive only: there is no time-dependent temperature model. Real skin temperature changes; fixing it isolates physical exchange.

Conduction is displayed but approximated as zero because shoe/ground contact is small. Internal conduction through clothing is already included in clothing resistance, and is not a second external heat loss. Respiration, obligatory skin diffusion, sweat-rate limits, wet-clothing effects and movement-generated airflow are omitted. Activity changes heat production only.

Equations actually used

The lecture’s S = M ± C ± R − E groups convection and conduction in C (lecture PDF page 26, slide 29). Internally dry fluxes are positive outward:

S = M − Cconv − K − R − E
Ereq = max(0, M − Cconv − K − R)
E = min(Ereq, Emax)

If dry losses exceed M, Ereq is zero and S remains negative: evaporation cannot warm the body. Otherwise balance is possible only if capacity meets demand and enough sweat is supplied. This is a feasibility calculation, not a feedback controller or a prediction of sweat secretion.

hc = max(3.0, 8.6 v^0.53)
Cconv = A fcl hc (Tcl − Ta)
R = A fcl fr ε σ [(Tcl + 273.15)^4 − (Ta + 273.15)^4]
A (Tskin − Tcl) / Rcl = Cconv + R
K = 0

The clothing surface temperature Tcl is solved by bisection between skin and air temperature. Convection and radiation therefore share one clothing resistance; it is not counted twice. The clothing is a uniform effective layer, and radiation uses absolute temperature in kelvin.

psat(T) = 0.6108 exp[17.27 T / (T + 237.3)] kPa
Δp = psat(33) − (RH / 100) psat(Ta)
Re = Rcl / (im LR) + 1 / (fcl LR hc)
Emax = max(0, A Δp / Re)

Capacity assumes a fully wet skin surface beneath the fixed clothing resistance. RH is relative to air temperature: even 100% RH can allow evaporation if the air is cooler than skin. When Δp < 0, evaporation is zero in V1; possible condensation heat is omitted and an explicit warning appears. Humidity constrains evaporation, not sweat production.

Constants, units & provenance

  • A = 1.8 m²; Tskin = 33°C: fixed curriculum/reference assumptions.
  • Rcl = 0.155 × 0.5 = 0.0775 m² K/W; fcl = 1 + 0.15 × 0.5 = 1.075; im = 0.45. Clothing area and permeability conventions, plus the natural/forced convection coefficient, follow the documented Gagge implementation. V1 borrows transport relationships, not its physiological controller. hc has units W/(m² K); v is m/s. The 3.0 floor approximates natural convection.
  • ε = 0.95; fr = 0.73; σ = 5.670374419 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m² K⁴); LR = 16.5 K/kPa. Radiation and vapour-resistance formulation are based on Cramer & Jay, Partitional calorimetry (2019). fr is an approximate standing radiating-area fraction. Re is in m² kPa/W, so A Δp/Re is W.
  • Saturation pressure: the FAO Tetens relationship, with T in °C and pressure in kPa.
  • M = 115, 230, 400 or 600 W: deliberately rounded teaching examples for rest through heavy activity, informed by the scale of metabolic workloads in NIOSH (2016). They are assigned net heat production, after external work, not measured rates or universal activity definitions.

The screen rounds to whole watts; calculations retain full precision. Independently rounded terms can differ by 1–2 W when added. “<1 W” identifies small nonzero flows. Numerical balance uses a 10⁻⁸ W tolerance, not a physiological comfort threshold.

At 33°C, convection and radiation vanish. Above it, both reverse. In hot dry air, wind increases both convective heat gain and evaporative capacity. In air with no evaporative capacity, extra wind increases heat gain instead. A small positive S is not rounded into a claim of physiological safety.

Known limits

All outputs are quantitatively approximate. This is a hybrid steady transport model, not a validated individual thermoregulation model. Fixed skin temperature becomes especially unrealistic in cold air. Full skin wettedness and unlimited sweat supply make capacity-based balance optimistic. Omitted respiratory heat loss and skin diffusion affect the budget, particularly during exercise and at rest. No sweat secretion rate, core temperature, exposure time, comfort score or clinical outcome is predicted.
